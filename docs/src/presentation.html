The problemIndian diabetes care is reactive. Spikes happen between visits.

  • ~101 million Indians live with diabetes and ~136 million with prediabetes (ICMR-INDIAB, Lancet D&E 2023).
  • HbA1c is checked every 3 months. Daily post-meal spikes, which drive complications, go unseen.
  • Indian physiology and lifestyle break Western models: the "thin-fat" phenotype, rice- and roti-heavy meals, late dinners, and festival fasting such as Navratri sabudana.
  • Wearables and CGMs now generate streams of data, but doctors get numbers, not foresight.
"What will this patient's glucose do in the next two hours, why, and what is the smallest change that prevents the spike?"

Sanjeevani answers this for every patient, continuously, from data a clinic already has or can get cheaply.

What we generate

  • 4 regional diets and a 28-food Indian library with glycaemic-index splits
  • Navratri 2026 fasting, weekend biryani, late IT-shift dinners
  • CGM (5 min), HR, HRV, sleep stages, steps; meal and medication logs
  • 6 story personas. All demo patients are held out from training

Why the evaluation is honest

  • The ground truth includes effects the twin cannot see: dawn phenomenon, unlogged snacks, ±22% carb-count error, day-to-day insulin-sensitivity drift, latent stress
  • CGM has 10-min lag, AR(1) noise, drift and dropouts
  • Patient-level split: 24 train, 6 calibration, 10 test
  • No real patient data. DPDP Act 2023 and HIPAA-safe

PersonalisationThe twin learns each patient's hidden physiology

  • EHR → Bayesian priors. BMI and waist (South-Asian weighting) give insulin sensitivity. Diabetes duration and TCF7L2 rs7903146 risk alleles give beta-cell function. Fasting glucose gives the hepatic set-point.
  • 3-day onboarding. A MAP cross-entropy search fits the twin to CGM in about 1 s per patient.
  • Live sync. A lag-compensated CGM observer keeps the twin's state current.

Why hybridPhysics makes AI data-efficient, quieter and explainable

−18%

error with only 2 training patients

2-h RMSE 31.3 vs 38.1 mg/dL for ML-only. The physics twin alone scores 34.4 with zero training patients, so it can start at a new clinic on day one.

−26%

false alarms

0.65 vs 0.88 per patient-day under the same alert policy, with higher precision (82% vs 79%). Alert fatigue is what kills clinical tools.

#1

feature group = the twin

Permutation importance: removing the twin's forecasts drops AUROC by 0.154, about 8× more than any wearable group alone.

Prediction intervals use conformalized quantile regression on disjoint calibration patients (76–78% observed coverage for a nominal 80%). Spike probabilities are isotonic-calibrated. A unit test proves features never use data from after the prediction time.

What-If LabPrescribe a simulation and see tomorrow

  • Sliders: post-meal walk, low-GI swap (millets, brown rice), earlier dinner, sleep, metformin dose
  • "Find the best plan" simulates 54 possible tomorrows in one batched run and ranks them by benefit per unit of effort
  • Rajesh's recommended plan: a 20-min walk after lunch and dinner plus a low-GI swap. Peak drops by 96 mg/dL, hyperglycaemia by 72%, and projected HbA1c falls 7.2% → 7.1% in 90 days

Ghost twinTwo futures, side by side, before the patient changes anything

  • In the What-If Lab the 3D view splits in two: current habits on the left and the prescribed plan on the right
  • Both bodies are time-lapsed through the next 24 h of simulated physiology. Blood colour, glucose numbers and the chart cursor move in sync
  • At 14:20 on Navratri: 273 mg/dL on current habits vs 212 mg/dL with the plan
  • Doctors and patients see the benefit. That is what drives behaviour change
